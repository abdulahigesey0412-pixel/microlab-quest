Lecture 4 · part 1

Arterial blood pressure: values and factors

Arterial blood pressure (ABP) is the pressure of blood on the arterial walls. The highest normal reading is 120/80 mmHg.

NormalMeaning
Systolic (SBP)120 mmHg (about 100–120)Pressure during left ventricular contraction; shows the pumping ability of the heart
Diastolic (DBP)80 mmHg (60–90)Pressure during left ventricular diastole; shows the peripheral resistance
Pulse pressure (PP)SBP − DBP = 40 (30–50)The difference between systolic and diastolic
The slide calls diastolic pressure the "maximum" pressure during diastole. Textbooks describe it as the lowest pressure in the cycle. If an exam option says "minimum pressure during diastole", that is the standard definition.

Physiological factors

FactorEffect
AgeNewborn 80/40 (vagal tone) · child 80/60 · adult 120/80 · then a gradual rise as arteries lose elasticity
SexBefore 45: males about 5 mmHg higher. After 45: diastolic higher in females
RaceAmericans and Europeans tend to have higher ABP
MealsDigestion increases ABP
Emotions and exerciseIncrease ABP through sympathetic activity
GravityEach 1 cm below the heart adds 0.77 mmHg; each 1 cm above subtracts 0.77 mmHg

What determines ABP

ABP = cardiac output (COP) × total peripheral resistance (TPR)

ChangeMain effectPulse pressure
↑ Stroke volumeRaises systolic more than diastolic↑
Change in heart rateMainly changes diastolic; systolic stays about the same—
↑ Peripheral resistanceRaises diastolic more than systolic↓
↓ Elasticity (atherosclerosis)Systolic ↑, diastolic ↓↑
"Systolic = Squeeze, Diastolic = Drag"Systolic shows how hard the heart Squeezes (stroke volume). Diastolic shows how much the vessels Drag (resistance). Stiff arteries split them apart: SBP up, DBP down, PP widens.
120 · 80 · 40 · 0.77SBP 120, DBP 80, PP 40. Every cm below the heart adds 0.77 mmHg.
↑ TPR raises DBP more, so pulse pressure narrows. Atherosclerosis raises SBP and lowers DBP, so pulse pressure widens. Heart-rate changes act mainly on diastolic pressure.

Lecture 4 · part 2

Regulation of arterial blood pressure

Three tiers: rapidly acting (nervous), intermediate, and slowly acting (the kidney).

1. Rapidly acting: medullary cardiovascular centres

AreaCentreEffect
Pressor area (vasomotor area)Vasoconstrictor centre (VCC): vasoconstrictor toneArteriolar constriction → ↑ TPR → ↑ ABP; venoconstriction → ↑ mean systemic filling pressure → ↑ venous return → ↑ COP
Cardiac stimulatory centre (CSC): sympathetic tone↑ HR and ↑ force of contraction → ↑ SV → ↑ COP and ABP
Depressor areaVasodilator centre (VDC)Inhibits the VCC → vasodilatation → ↓ peripheral resistance
Cardiac inhibitory centre (CIC)Inhibits the heart through the vagus (vagal tone) → bradycardia → ↓ COP
Baroreceptor reflexChemoreceptor reflex
ReceptorsCarotid sinus and aortic archCarotid body and aortic body
StimulusABP changes between 60 and 200 mmHgHypoxia (main stimulus); ABP between 40 and 60 mmHg (e.g. haemorrhage, with acidosis and hypercapnia)
Response↑ ABP → inhibits the pressor area → ↓ HR, vasodilatation. ↓ ABP → the reverseMainly stimulates respiration; excites the pressor area and inhibits the depressor area → ↑ ABP
  • Afferent nerves: glossopharyngeal (IX) via the carotid sinus nerve, and vagus (X) from the aortic arch.
  • Centre: vasomotor centre in the medulla oblongata.
  • Timing: starts within seconds, lasts hours; nervous.
  • Other cardiovascular receptors on the slide: low-pressure atrial and ventricular receptors, the coronary chemoreflex (Bezold-Jarisch) and pulmonary juxtacapillary (J) receptors.

2. Intermediate: capillary fluid shift (within minutes)

  • ABP falls → capillary hydrostatic pressure falls → fluid moves from tissues into capillaries → ↑ venous return → ↑ COP → ↑ ABP.
  • ABP rises → more filtration out of capillaries → ↓ venous return and COP → ↓ ABP.
  • The slide also lists stress-relaxation of vessels as an intermediate mechanism.

3. Slowly acting: the kidney

  1. Pressure diuresis.
  2. Renin–angiotensin system: falling BP → less renal blood flow → kidney ischaemia → the juxtaglomerular apparatus releases renin → renin converts angiotensinogen (renin substrate, from the liver) into angiotensin I → ACE in the lung converts it to angiotensin II, a very powerful vasoconstrictor → ↑ BP.
  3. Aldosterone: falling BP → adrenal cortex releases aldosterone → sodium and water retention → ↑ blood volume → ↑ BP.
Seconds · Minutes · DaysNerves act in seconds, capillaries in minutes, the kidney slowly. "Liver makes the Lock (angiotensinogen), Kidney makes the Key (renin), Lung Locks it on (ACE)."
Baro = Big range, Chemo = Crisis rangeBaroreceptors work from 60 to 200 mmHg; chemoreceptors join in at 40–60 mmHg, when hypoxia appears. Sinus → IX, arch → X.
Baroreceptors are in the carotid sinus; chemoreceptors are in the carotid body. ACE is in the lung; renin comes from the juxtaglomerular apparatus. The chemoreceptors' main job is to stimulate respiration.

Lecture 5 · part 1

Endocrine basics, the pituitary and growth hormone

  • Endocrinology studies glands of internal secretion. Hormones are chemicals secreted into body fluids and delivered directly into the blood. Exocrine glands secrete through ducts.
  • The pituitary (hypophysis) lies in the sella turcica beneath the brain.
PartCellsHormones
Posterior pituitary (neurohypophysis)Stores hormones made in the hypothalamusADH (vasopressin), oxytocin
Intermediate lobeRudimentary in humans
Anterior pituitary (adenohypophysis)Chromophobes, 50%Precursors of alpha or beta cells
Acidophils (alpha), 40%GH, prolactin (luteotropic hormone, LTH)
Basophils (beta), 10%TSH, ACTH, LH, FSH

Growth hormone (somatotropin)

  • Secreted by the anterior pituitary (acidophils). It has direct effects and an indirect effect, by stimulating the liver to make somatomedin (IGF-1).
  • Growth: in children, before the epiphyses close, it causes linear growth. In adults, after closure, bones grow in thickness only.
ProteinCarbohydrateFat
↑ protein synthesis (anabolic)↑ blood glucose↑ lipolysis, ↑ blood free fatty acids and ketone bodies
Acidophils make "GP": GH and ProlactinBasophils make "B-FLAT": FSH, LH, ACTH, TSH. Cell shares 50 · 40 · 10 (chromophobes, acidophils, basophils).
GH builds protein, spares sugar, burns fatProtein up, glucose up, lipolysis up, ketones up.
Prolactin comes from acidophils, not basophils. ADH and oxytocin are stored and released by the posterior pituitary. After epiphyseal closure GH thickens bone but cannot make it longer.

Lecture 5 · part 2

GH disorders, ADH and oxytocin

DisorderCauseKey features
Gigantism↑ GH from acidophils before epiphyseal unionAll tissues grow fast → giant; high BMR; hyperglycaemia and glucosuria ("pituitary diabetes", not sensitive to insulin); lowered resistance; hypogonadism from acidophils pressing on basophils
Acromegaly↑ GH after epiphyseal unionCoarse face; big ears and nose (cartilage); bones thicken in width: protruding jaws, prominent supraorbital ridge, separation of teeth; big hands and feet; kyphosis; soft-tissue overgrowth (lips, tongue, liver, spleen); hypogonadism; ↑ BMR, hyperglycaemia, glucosuria
Dwarfism↓ GH in childhoodShort but proportionate (span = height, upper = lower segment); at 20 looks 10; no mental retardation; no thyroid or adrenal deficiency; hypogonadism
Infantilism↓ GH and sex hormonesShort stature, normal mentality, no sexual development
Dental angle: acromegaly separates the teeth and enlarges the jaw and tongue, so a patient's dentures or bite can change. Cretinism (Lecture 6) delays tooth eruption.

Posterior pituitary

ADH (vasopressin)

  • ↑ water reabsorption by the kidney → ↓ water excretion → ↓ urine volume.
  • ↑ ABP.
  • Deficiency: diabetes insipidus (polyuria).

Oxytocin

  • Contracts the uterus during delivery.
  • Milk ejection reflex: baby suckles → sensory impulses from the nipple → oxytocin in blood → contraction of myoepithelial cells → milk flows.
Before the plate = Giant, After the plate = AcroSame excess GH; the epiphyseal plate decides: open = taller, closed = thicker ("acro" = extremities).
Dwarf = Wise and in proportion; Infantile = No pubertyDwarfism: small, proportionate, normal mind. Infantilism adds missing sex hormones.
ADH = "Add Dat H₂O"Keeps water, so urine falls. No ADH = diabetes insipidus, floods of dilute urine.
Both gigantism and acromegaly cause hyperglycaemia and hypogonadism. Dwarfism has no mental retardation (that is cretinism). Diabetes insipidus is an ADH problem, not insulin.

Lecture 6 · part 1

Thyroid hormones, control and actions

  • Follicular cells secrete thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3). The follicle lumen holds colloid, a glycoprotein.
  • Parafollicular (C) cells secrete calcitonin.
  • Control: hypothalamus TRH → anterior pituitary TSH → thyroid T4, T3. T4 feeds back to ↓ TSH.
SystemAction of thyroid hormone
General metabolism↑ BMR, O₂ consumption and heat production
Carbohydrate↑ blood glucose (gluconeogenesis, glucose utilisation)
Lipids↓ blood cholesterol (more conversion to bile acids and uptake from plasma); ↑ fatty acid mobilisation
ProteinAnabolic for energy production (catabolic in muscle at high levels)
CNSGrowth, differentiation and maturation
CVS↑ heart rate and cardiac output
GIT↑ appetite, motility and secretions
Pituitary and bone↓ TSH, ↑ GH; ↑ bone growth, maturation and resorption
Thyroid hormone = the body's acceleratorEverything speeds up: metabolism, heat, heart, gut, brain development. Only cholesterol and TSH go down.
T-T-T: TRH → TSH → T4Hypothalamus, pituitary, thyroid. T4 switches TSH off.
Calcitonin comes from parafollicular C cells, not follicular cells. Thyroid hormone lowers cholesterol, so hypothyroidism raises it.

Lecture 6 · part 2

Hyperthyroidism, myxoedema and cretinism

Hyperthyroidism (thyrotoxicosis, Graves' disease)

Cause: excess hormone production due to LATS (long-acting thyroid stimulator): abnormal antibodies made by lymphocytes in an autoimmune disease, stimulating the gland with a long-acting effect.

Clinical picture: heat intolerance, fine straight hair, bulging eyes, facial flushing, enlarged thyroid, tachycardia, ↑ systolic BP, clubbing, breast enlargement, weight loss, tremors, muscle wasting, diarrhoea, menstrual changes (amenorrhoea), localised oedema.

Hypothyroidism

Adult: myxoedema

Causes:

  1. Primary: damage or surgical removal of the thyroid; metabolic defect.
  2. Secondary: ↓ TSH (e.g. Simmond's disease).
  3. Tertiary: hypothalamic lesion.

Picture: cold intolerance, facial and eyelid oedema, dull expression, hair loss, fatigue, apathy, thick tongue, slow speech, hoarse voice, dry coarse skin, brittle nails, poor appetite, constipation, weight gain, poor memory, muscle aches, menstrual problems, high cholesterol. Late: subnormal temperature, bradycardia, thick skin, ↓ consciousness, cardiac complications.

Child: cretinism

Causes (in utero or early life): absent thyroid gland, or a metabolic defect so the gland cannot make hormone.

Usually detected at 6 months. Dull, apathetic infant, coarse face, hoarse cry; delayed bone growth → short, depressed nose; delayed sitting, walking and tooth eruption; defective speech; dry skin, scanty hair; mental retardation (the most common cause of curable mental retardation); soft tissue grows more than bone → big liver and gut, weak abdominal wall → pot belly and umbilical hernia, protruding tongue → open mouth; ↓↓↓ BMR with hypothermia; incontinence; infantilism, sterility; ↑↑↑ cholesterol.

The feedback table is worked out from the TRH → TSH → T4 loop in the lecture, so you can reason through "predict the change" questions.

Hyper = Hot, Hungry, Hurried · Hypo = Cold, Constipated, CoarseHyper: heat intolerance, weight loss with appetite, tachycardia, tremor, diarrhoea. Hypo: cold intolerance, constipation, coarse skin and voice, bradycardia, weight gain.
Cretin's "5 P's": Pot belly, Protruding tongue, Puffy face, Poor growth, Poor mindAnd it is curable if found early, which is why newborn screening matters.
LATS is an antibody from lymphocytes, not a pituitary hormone, so TSH is low in Graves'. Secondary hypothyroidism has low TSH; primary has high TSH. Dwarfism has normal intelligence; cretinism has mental retardation.

How questions catch you

Trick 1Swapped partners

Sinus vs body, acidophil vs basophil, gigantism vs acromegaly, dwarfism vs cretinism, primary vs secondary.

Trick 2Wrong direction

Does pulse pressure widen or narrow? Does cholesterol rise or fall? Does TSH go up or down?

Trick 3Wrong organ

Renin from the kidney, angiotensinogen from the liver, ACE in the lung, aldosterone from the adrenal cortex.

Trick 4Wrong number

60–200 vs 40–60 mmHg; 50/40/10%; 0.77 mmHg per cm; 6 months for cretinism.

Trick 5Wrong timing

Seconds vs minutes vs slow; before vs after epiphyseal union.

Trick 6Look-alike disease

Diabetes insipidus is ADH; "pituitary diabetes" is the hyperglycaemia of GH excess.
